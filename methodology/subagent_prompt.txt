ELIGIBILITY

A mystery work is fiction whose story centrally creates a sustained desire in its audience to determine or understand something that is not initially known. A conventional detective, crime, or explicit investigation is not required. Prefer fiction, though an exceptional nonfiction work is eligible when it genuinely functions as a mystery for its audience. Unresolved mysteries remain eligible, but failure to resolve questions the work strongly promises to answer normally reduces fairness unless incompleteness is clearly part of the established expectations.

WORK-UNIT NORMALIZATION

THE FOUR SCORES

Scores are absolute evaluations, not transformed ranks. Use the full 0–100 scale with discrimination. Membership in any top-100 selection list proves strength on the selecting axis only; it does not imply that the work deserves high scores on the other axes. When a selected work is substantially lacking on another axis, score that weakness candidly and use the lower portions of the scale.

Use these anchors consistently:

- 0: essentially none of the relevant quality.
- 25: weak or seriously deficient.
- 50: adequate, mixed, or ordinary achievement.
- 75: very good and clearly above average.
- 90: exceptional.
- 100: perfection or the most defensible possible ceiling case.

A score of 0 is a deliberate judgment and must never mean “not ranked on this axis.” A score of 50 is not a failing score. A score of 100 should be rare and should not merely mean “one of the best works I selected.” Do not compress nearly all scores into the 80–100 range simply because every union work is strong somewhere. At the same time, do not force a target average or a predetermined number of low scores; evaluate each work honestly on each axis.

1. INFLUENCE — Importance in the landscape of mystery and in later works inside or outside the genre that drew from it. This is distinct from present-day quality and present-day originality. Measure it for every union work, but never use it to select candidates or determine placement on the three submitted lists.

2. AMBITION — Grandeur, complexity, scope, and the challenge of coherently managing and resolving the work’s questions, structures, perspectives, layers, and moving parts.

3. FAIRNESS — Whether revelations and solutions follow from rules, evidence, mechanisms, and expectations established beforehand. Reward retrospectively legible construction and earned solutions. Penalize unsupported culprits, newly introduced mechanics, arbitrary escapes, and strongly promised questions that are simply abandoned. Apply the principle to the form of mystery the work actually attempts.

4. ORIGINALITY — How distinctive the work feels in its setting, central questions, construction, devices, and resolution. Consider both historical novelty and distinctiveness to a contemporary audience; heavily copied formulas may now feel conventional.
